YOU'RE INVITED

OLD FRIENDS, NEW PHYSICS.

On the evening of December 13, 2026, the US Muon Collider Collaboration opens
its annual meeting at Stanford with a reunion — for the colleagues we miss
from the good old days, and for the people they work with now.

Come for the cocktail hour, with a drink in your hand and a room that speaks
your language. Stay for the research fair, where our community lays out what a
10 TeV muon collider takes to build and where the interesting problems are.
Arguments welcome.

The evening has a headliner. None other than Michael Peskin will give remarks
on why reaching the 10 TeV interaction scale is a crucial frontier for us to
explore — delivered with a drink in hand.

  if   you miss the friends and the physics of your HEP days

THREE THINGS, BEFORE YOU CLOSE THIS

1. RSVP. It takes a minute, and it tells us how big a room to hold.

2. Pass this along to five HEP alumni friends. A note from you carries further
   than anything we can post — the postdoc who went to a trading desk, the
   instrumentation wizard now building chips, the friend whose thesis you
   still cite.

3. Sponsor a round. Contributions at RSVP and support from organizations keep
   the evening open to every guest and help send students to the meeting.
   Just reply.

A 10 TeV machine will be built by people who have done hard things before, and
by the ones they bring with them. We would like to see you there.
